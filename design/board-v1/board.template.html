De-Saturator

Three directions for the plugin and its brand, each one a working window. Turn Amount, draw on the analyser, and try Delta, Live, Character and Bypass to see what each look does with them.

The analyser is fed by a stand-in signal. Nothing plays.

A

Grey card

A photographer holds up a grey card to take the colour cast out of a picture. Here the whole window is that card, and the only colour on it is what the plugin takes out: the orange between the original and the result, and the arc on the Amount knob. Bypass it and the window turns fully grey.

Drag a knob up or down (Shift for fine; double-click resets). On the analyser: double-click to add a point, drag it, scroll on it to change its width, double-click it to remove it. With the window focused, D toggles Delta and B Bypass.

Mark

A disc half on the card: the half on the card has lost its colour and left only its outline. It also reads as a D.

De-Saturator

All the parent's: graphite is the card, ink the screen and knobs, signal is what gets removed. Anybody ExtraBold for the name, Anybody for labels, Departure Mono for values.

How the states look

What's removed
Orange, filled between the original (the faint line) and the result. Orange means removal and nothing else.
Delta
The orange stays and everything else dims, so what you see in orange is what you hear.
Live
Your curve turns dashed and the smoothed curve Live applies is drawn solid over it.
Bypassed
No orange anywhere, not even in the logo.

Watch: orange and graphite are close in lightness, so every orange shape sits on ink or is edged in ink: the screen, the knob's track, the Delta button.

B

Hairline

Thinner, drawn literally. The analyser is a comb of lines: what stays is drawn heavy, and what's removed carries on as a hairline up to where the original reached. The Amount knob thins the same way, from a solid disc at 0 to a hairline ring at 100, and the name loses weight letter by letter.

Turn Amount and watch the knob's ring thin. The same controls as above.

Mark

One sine wave, the parent's own part, losing weight from left to right.

How the states look

What's removed
The hairline tip of each line. The heavy part is what you keep.
Delta
The weights swap: the removed part turns heavy and klein, the rest drops to a hairline.
Live
As in A: your curve dashed, the applied one solid.
Bypassed
Every line heavy, end to end. Nothing thins.

Watch: hairlines fade at 75% on a low-resolution screen. The thin part keeps a one-pixel floor, and the comb stays at 120 lines.

C

Halftone

Saturation the way a printer means it: how much ink is on the page. The analyser is a halftone screen in columns. Paper dots are what stays, pink dots are the ink being lifted off, and the Amount knob's own dot screen gets finer as you turn it up.

The dot screen updates at 30 frames a second, like a hardware meter. The same controls as above.

Mark

A disc of halftone dots that shrink to nothing, the lifted half in pink.

What they share

  • One layout: the analyser and its curve on the left, Amount on the right with Restore, Mix and Output under it, Shape beneath the analyser, and the modes and listening switches in the header.
  • The window is 960 × 600 and scales from 75% to 200% at that ratio. Everything is shapes and strokes, so nothing needs a bitmap.
  • The thinning curve is a set of bells on a line at 100% of Amount, up to six. The top is 200%, the bottom leaves that range untouched.
  • The result is drawn before Output and Auto gain, so the gap only ever shows what was removed, never a change in level.
  • In Live, the curve you draw and the smoothed curve that's applied are both shown. In HQ they are the same line. The latency label holds up to three digits.
  • Type is the parent brand's: Anybody for the name and labels, Departure Mono for values. Both are under the SIL Open Font License, so they can ship inside a free plugin.
